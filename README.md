# Stocks

One of the app's features lets you rank every stock of Nasdaq, NYSE and Cboe on the numbers that matter to a long-term holder: dividend yield, dividend growth, payout and FCF payout, quick and current ratios, debt to equity and net debt to EBITDA, revenue, EPS, FCF, EBITDA, CapEx, asset and debt growth, net, gross, EBITDA and FCF margins with their year-over-year change, ROIC, valuation multiples (P/E, forward P/E, P/S, forward P/S, P/FCF, EV/EBITDA, FCF/EBITDA) short-term momentum (1 to 60 trading days, 1 month, 1 quarter, week, month and quarter to date, 2 weeks to 52 weeks) and performance and total returns over 1, 3, 5 and 10 years with CAGRs. Another feature lets you tick stocks into a Watchlist, open per-stock Fundamentals, Dividends and History tabs, and copy or export the tickers. A client-side tool (Parcel + Tailwind CSS v4, built into `dist`) that reads the generated `./api/stocks` static feed (SEC EDGAR XBRL annual filings, SEC company ticker table, Yahoo Finance prices, dividends and valuation snapshot) into a searchable stock catalog - the same look, feel and interaction model as the ETFs applications, with no runtime dependencies

## Using Bun

```bash
bunx degit daggerok/Stocks#main ./12345 && cd $_
bun install
./scripts/update-data.ts --restore-from
bun run serve
open http://0:1234
```

`api/stocks` is not in git: `./scripts/update-data.ts --restore-from` downloads the published feed from <https://daggerok.github.io/Stocks/api/stocks/> into an empty `api/stocks` once (see [Publishing](#publishing)). Without it the app builds and shows an empty catalog

`bun run serve` is the Parcel dev server (it copies `api/`, the base presets `src/core/screener/presets`, `user-data/` and the scoring config `src/core/scoring/config.json` as `scoring.json` into `dist` first); `bun run build` writes the production site to `dist` and `bun run build-github-pages` does the same for the `/Stocks/` public URL

The published application is available at <https://daggerok.github.io/Stocks/>

### Column types and filters

Every column of the catalog and of the Fundamentals, Dividends and History tabs has a type: text (`ABC`), number (`123`), percentage (`%`), money (`$`), date (`D`), date and time (`DT`) or time of day (`T`). The type is detected from the texts the column shows (80% of the filled cells must agree, otherwise text) and is written in the badge next to the column title: click it to cycle the type, Shift+click to return to auto-detection. Dates are read as `2024-06-15`, `6/15/2024`, `15.06.2024`, `Jun 15, 2024` or `15-Jun-2024`, date and time as `2024-06-15T09:30:00Z` or `2024-06-15 09:30`, time as `09:30`, `16:00:00` or `9:30 PM`

A row of filter inputs sits under the column headers (the `Filters` button hides it, `Clear filters` empties it). Filters of different columns are combined with AND, the Search box and the Exchanges, Sectors and Hide stale controls apply on top, and Copy Tickers and the exports use the filtered rows. Filters and type overrides are remembered in the browser; filters keep working on columns that are hidden in the Columns menu (hiding a column only hides it from the table). The `Columns` menu lists every column one by one (Use and Ticker are listed but locked, everything else is shown by default, with search, All, Clear, Toggle and Reset) and the choice is remembered in the browser. `Sticky #` (next to `Filters`, off by default, remembered in the browser) numbers the rows by their rank in the table sorted by the current column before the column filters, so a filtered stock keeps its rank and the numbers keep gaps; the sort, the search, the Watchlist and the Exchanges, Sectors, blacklist and Hide stale choices rank again. Every export starts with the `#` column. The red `Clear` button opens a dialog with a checkbox for everything it can reset (selected stocks, searches, sorts, open tab, exchanges and sectors, shown columns, hide stale, column filters and types, Filters on/off, Sticky #, scroll positions, the blacklist and the screener choice; the theme, the language and own presets are kept): all ticked the first time, afterwards as left at the last OK. Enter is OK, Esc is Cancel. The ticked items go back to the first-visit view (also after a reload)

Inside one filter: a space means AND, a comma means OR, a leading `!` means NOT, `?` matches an empty or unavailable value and `!?` a value that is there; a value that is unavailable matches only `?` and negated conditions. An unquoted space ends the value, so quote values that contain one (`>="2024-06-15 09:30"`)

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Text | `bank` contains, `"two words"`, `!bank`, `=exact`, `^starts`, `ends$`, `/regex/`, `tech, health` |
| Number, percentage, money | `>10`, `>=10 <50`, `=22` (matches what rounds to 22), `!=22`, `10..50`, `..50`, `10..`, `>1B` and `K` `M` `B` `T` suffixes, an optional `$` or `%` |
| Date, date and time | `>2024-06-01`, `2024` (the whole year), `2024-06` (the whole month), `2024-01..2024-06`, `today`, `yesterday`, `-7d..` (the last 7 days), `+2w`, `-3m`, `-1y` |
| Time | `>09:30`, `09:30..16:00`, `=12:00` (the whole minute) |

### Screener, guide and language

The Screener panel above the table narrows the same table, it does not open a second one:

- `Stocks`, `ETFs`, `All` picks the securities. ETFs are read directly from the 29 brand feeds of the ETFs hub (`https://daggerok.github.io/<Brand>/api/<slug>/index.json`) the first time they are needed. The brand list is not copied here: the app reads it at run time from the hub's `https://daggerok.github.io/ETFs/brands.json` (repo, brand, slug, issuer label and URL per brand; checked by `parseEtfBrands` in `src/core/screener/etf-brands.ts`). A brand that fails is reported and left out; when `brands.json` itself fails, the stocks still load and the screener status names the error. Stock-only columns stay empty for ETFs. "1-year return" means the total return `tr1y` for stocks and ETFs alike (Up over 1 year is `tr1y > 0`); `perf1y` is the separate price return and stays empty for ETFs. The sector of an ETF is its fund category from the brand feed: the sector scope and the sector percentiles of the scores compare ETFs only with ETFs of the same category
- Preset chips: click to use a preset, `¬` to exclude it (NOT), `▾` to open its conditions. `All of` and `Any of` combine the chosen presets with AND or OR; the refinements after "and" (Pays a dividend, Up over 1 year) always apply on top, so "Gold + dividend yield > 0" is two clicks and "Quality growth or Growth without profit + 1-year return > 0" is four. A preset that also selects ETFs (Gold, Mine) switches the table from Stocks to All
- The editor shows each condition with its metric (searchable), operator, value (a list for `in`), comparison group, empty-value rule and on/off switch, `+ metric`, the match mode (all or "N of M") and the funnel: how many rows can still match after each condition and where the selection empties. Edited conditions are highlighted; `Reset`, `Save as my preset`, `Export` (`<id>.json` for `user-data/presets/`), `Import` and `Copy link` (the preset and its `schemaVersion` in the link) work without a backend. The `Presets` column lists the presets each row passes
- Base presets are fetched from `./presets/` (built from `src/core/screener/presets`), own presets from `./user-data/presets/` and peer groups from `./user-data/peers.json`; each folder has an `index.json` with its file list (a test keeps it in sync). Own presets saved in the browser win over the repo copies and are marked "not exported" until exported or committed
- Scores, archetype, status, Why and flags (plan stage 5, `src/core/scoring`): the columns `Archetype`, `Status`, `Why`, `Flags` and `Score`, `Cheap`, `Quality`, `Safety`, `Dividend`, `Entry` (0-100, colored green from 70 and red below 40). Each score is the weighted mean of sector percentiles of its metrics (an ETF ranks inside its fund category; a sector or category with fewer than 20 values compares with the stock market or with all ETFs; ETFs get only Entry and Dividend), the archetype (stable, growth, volatile) comes from ordered rules (a REIT is growth only by approximate FFO growth, `isReit` comes from the Yahoo industry), a flag rule may carry further `or` groups (the main red flag also fires on cash conversion below 0.5 while net income grows), a stop factor of the archetype gives Rejected, otherwise the weighted score against the archetype's thresholds gives Buy zone, Watch or Rejected; Why lists the reasons in the current language. Every weight, threshold, rule, flag and note is in `src/core/scoring/config.json` (served as `scoring.json`) and changes without code; the guide sections Five scores, Archetypes and statuses, Flags and Thresholds explain them. The outputs are also metrics the presets can filter on (`status`, `archetype`, `scoreSafety`, `flagCount`)
- Peer groups (plan stage 6): `Peer groups` in the Screener panel edits the groups of the peers scope: create a group, add tickers with Enter, remove them, delete a group. `user-data/peers.json` is the starting state, the browser copy wins and is marked "not exported" until `Export peers.json` (the whole file, ready to replace `user-data/peers.json`); `Import` reads such a file back. A stock without a group is compared with its industry
- Ticker page (plan stage 7): a ticker in the table opens its page with eight sections. `Overview`: key figures, the five scores colored, archetype, status, Why and flags. `Statements`: income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement for up to five fiscal years plus TTM with the change against the year before, from the SEC layer of `meta.json` (a foreign issuer can switch to its USD view; an older feed falls back to the annual rows). `Long-run charts`: revenue, net income, operating and free cash flow and the four margins as plain SVG. `Peers`: market cap, revenue, net income, FCF, P/E, both PEGs, P/B and P/S of the peer group, else of the largest stocks of the industry, absolutes in USD. `DCF`: the FCF and EPS flows side by side (g1 for years 1-5, g2 for 6-10, WACC 9.5% and terminal growth 3% by default, WACC at or below the terminal growth rejected), bear, base and bull scenarios, the margin of safety at the price, a flag when the two values diverge by more than 30% and the capex intensity; the EPS flow base is the reported EPS or operating income after tax per share (effective tax rate within 0-50%, else 21%); edited inputs stay in the browser and export and import as `user-data/dcf/<TICKER>.json`. `TAM`: market size, growth and three market shares turned into the revenue at the horizon and the growth it needs. `Research notes`: a dated one-paragraph thesis, the nine-question checklist and the management promises tracker (buybacks against the shares actually retired and capex against the capex spent, from the SEC quarters). TAM, notes and promises live in the browser, export to `user-data/tam/<T>.json`, `user-data/notes/<T>.md` and `user-data/promises/<T>.json` and are read back from there after a commit
- Similar to X: the `Similar` section of the ticker page lists the 10 stocks closest to it on one metric group (Business, Dividend, Valuation, Risk from `src/core/similar/config.json`, served as `similar.json` and fetched like `scoring.json`, or an own set of registry keys): percentiles inside the sector when it has at least 20 rows (`minSectorRows`; candidates from the sector only), else among all stocks, the root mean square gap over the metrics both have (an empty metric is left out pair by pair, a stock needs half of the usable metrics in common), similarity = 100 - gap, the three largest differences per stock. `Add to peer group` puts the stock and the picked rows into a group, `Make a filter from X` turns it into an own preset of range conditions (its values ±20%)
- Signals (plan stage 8): `Signals` in the top panel lists what changed from one data run to the next, read from `api/stocks/signals.json` (last 12 weeks): a stock entered or left a base or committed own preset (an exit only after two runs in a row with fresh data), a flag of the scoring config appeared or went away, the status or the archetype changed, a score moved by 15 points or more. Filters by date and type; a ticker opens its page. A stock whose source failed in the run, or that the run did not refresh, gives no signals; a change with unchanged fiscal period ends but moved SEC values is marked as restated past periods. Links: `#signals` opens the view, `#ticker=AAPL` the ticker page. The update workflow runs the updater with `--signals`, restores `signals.json` together with the feed (Actions cache, else `--restore-from` the site) and publishes it in the same Pages artifact; a scheduled (weekly) run then opens one GitHub issue "Signals for <period>" with the signals of the last 7 days and links to the site, assigned to the owner (job `signals-issue`, the only one with `issues: write`; `--signals-issue <dir>` writes its text)
- `EN` and `RU` in the top panel switch the language of the whole page: the guide, the metric reference, the table headers and tooltips (name and short text of the metric registry), the screener, the toolbar, filters, the Watchlist and detail tabs, the Columns menu, the Clear dialog, the ticker page and the export labels. English is the default; every UI string goes through `t()` (`src/core/i18n`, the page chrome as `ui.<English text>` keys used by `L()` in `main.tsx`, static texts of `index.html` marked with `data-i18n` attributes). `Guide` opens every metric of the registry grouped (valuation, quality, safety, dividends, growth, price and swings, ETF data, fields, scores) with formula, short text and article, planned metrics marked, plus how presets, scopes and percentiles, empty values and the soft mode, and own data work, with a search box

## Updating the static Stocks data

Run the updater with Bun:

```bash
bun test
./scripts/update-data.ts
```

Run `./scripts/update-data.ts -h` (or `--help`) to print every configuration variable with its default and usage examples

### Publishing

The feed is not committed: `api/stocks/` is in `.gitignore` and lives in two places, the published site and the Actions cache. The **Update Stocks data** workflow (weekly and manual)

1. restores the previous `api/stocks` from the Actions cache (`feed-stocks-*`), and when there is none downloads the published one with `./scripts/update-data.ts --restore-from`, so the never-shrinking index and the rotation cursor (`update-state.json`) keep working
2. runs the updater as before, then saves `api/stocks` to the cache (also after a partly failed run: every stock is either fully updated or kept)
3. builds the site (`bun run build-github-pages` copies `api` into `dist`), refuses to deploy when `dist/api/stocks/index.json` is missing or lists no companies, and deploys `dist` as a GitHub Pages artifact

A code change pushed to `main` (the **GitHub Pages** workflow) restores the feed the same way before it builds, with the same guard, so it never replaces the site with one that has no data. The cache is evicted after 7 days without use; the site is the fallback, so a lost cache costs one download

Locally the updater still writes to `api/stocks`. `./scripts/update-data.ts --restore-from [url]` fills an empty `api/stocks` from the published feed (or another `.../api/stocks/` URL) and refuses to overwrite a local `index.json`: to start over, delete `api/stocks` first. A local run is not published; run the workflow for that

Defaults live in `scripts/update-data.config.json` (every control as a string). An explicitly set environment variable overrides the file, even when it is empty. The **Update Stocks data** GitHub Actions workflow uses the same resolver (`resolveControls` in `scripts/update-data.ts`): individual `workflow_dispatch` inputs are blank by default and inherit the file, and the `advanced` input accepts a JSON object with any control (for example `{"VERBOSE":"true"}`). Precedence: file defaults < advanced JSON < nonblank inputs < protected Actions variable or environment. `SEC_UA` and `USE_SYSTEM_CA` are set through `advanced`, and `SEC_UA` is also taken from the protected `SEC_UA` repository Actions variable when it is nonblank. The workflow always writes to `api/stocks` only and publishes it as a Pages artifact (see [Publishing](#publishing)). All supplied filters use **AND** logic

### Watchlist

`scripts/All.txt` (or, if it is missing or lists nothing, `scripts/All.csv`) is the owner's list. It does not narrow the feed (plan 1.1): a run without `TICKERS` covers every stock on `EXCHANGES`. The list marks the owner's stocks and puts them first in every full-universe run, so they are refreshed every week while the rotation cursor works through the rest of the universe; a stock that is not in the list is never removed because of it

- `All.txt`: tickers separated by new lines, spaces, commas or semicolons; `All.csv`: the first cell of each row, a `Symbol` or `Ticker` header is ignored
- Spellings are matched to the SEC table: an exchange prefix is dropped (`NYSE:TTE` -> `TTE`) and `BRK.B`, `BRK/B` and `BRK-B` are the same ticker. Then the `TICKER_ALIASES` table at the top of `scripts/update-data.ts` maps known spellings (`BRKB` -> `BRK-B`); grow it when a new spelling shows up. Without an entry, a ticker that is not in the table as written matches the one listed ticker with the same letters, an ambiguous one is reported as not found
- ETFs and funds in the list are not in the SEC table or are reported by Yahoo as funds, so they are not stock rows (they still count for `mineTickers`); a preferred share, warrant or unit in the list is updated, unlike the rest of the universe
- `TICKERS` runs exactly the given tickers, inside or outside the list, and never moves the rotation cursor
- Every published row carries `isMine` (true for the tickers of the list, false for all rows without a list) and `index.json` lists every ticker of the file, ETFs included, in `mineTickers`: the screener's "Mine" preset reads them

### Data sources

| Block | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Universe, exchange, CIK | SEC `https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers_exchange.json` (Nasdaq, NYSE and Cboe rows; OTC and unlisted rows are ignored) |
| Annual fundamentals | SEC EDGAR XBRL `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json`, 10-K facts only, up to 15 fiscal years |
| Quarters and TTM | The same companyfacts, 10-K and 10-Q (`us-gaap`), parsed by `scripts/sec/companyfacts.ts` into 10 fiscal years, 40 discrete quarters and TTM, stored in `meta.json` under `fundamentals.sec` as arrays by field (newest first) with `end`, `start`, `fiscalYear`, `period`, `filed` and `units`; periods come from fact dates and durations (never `fy` and `fp`), the latest `filed` wins, a quarter is the 3-month fact or year-to-date minus the previous year-to-date, Q4 = fiscal year - 9 months, TTM = last fiscal year + year-to-date - the same year-to-date a year earlier, balance lines of the last quarter; share counts and per-share values filed before a split are restated with the Yahoo split ratio. An issuer that has tagged no nonzero debt line for 3 years before its latest fiscal year end is debt-free: debt 0 at every period (a missing debt fact at one date of an issuer with debt stays empty). An issuer without a diluted EPS and diluted share count in companyfacts (Visa reports both per share class) gets the diluted shares of all classes from the XBRL instance of the filing behind its TTM (`scripts/sec/shares.ts`, two EDGAR requests, reused from the published layer on the next run): a class whose EPS x shares equals net income is as-converted and stands for all, else the classes add up |
| Foreign issuers (ADRs) | The same companyfacts, forms 20-F, 40-F and 6-K: `us-gaap` tags first, then their `ifrs-full` synonyms (`scripts/sec/tags.ts`); the reporting currency is the majority unit of the latest annual filing (a USD convenience translation does not count); a period reported in both taxonomies takes the value of the latest filing (Toyota's IFRS restatement of FY2020 wins over its earlier US GAAP value), a tie keeps `us-gaap`. Most 20-F filers have no quarterly XBRL, so TTM is the last fiscal year (`ttmIsAnnual`). Amounts are converted to USD with the Yahoo chart of `<CUR>USD=X` (else the inverted `USD<CUR>=X`), loaded once per currency and run (`scripts/sec/fx.ts`): balance lines at the period-end rate, flows and per-share values at the period average; the USD layer is stored next to the SEC layer as `fundamentals.usd` with the rates applied. A foreign layer whose TTM ends more than 15 months before the last close is not used (the Yahoo fallback stays) |
| Fundamentals gap filler | Yahoo Finance `fundamentals-timeseries` (about 4 years): fills tags SEC does not carry (for a foreign issuer only points in its reporting currency) and covers issuers without usable SEC facts and holding-company reorganizations |
| Price history, dividends, returns | Yahoo Finance public chart API (`/v8/finance/chart/{TICKER}?interval=1d&events=div,split`) |
| Market cap, TTM and forward valuation, sector, analyst estimates | Yahoo Finance `quoteSummary` (needs a cookie and a crumb, opened once per run; a failed session leaves the forward multiples unavailable and keeps the published estimates); `earningsTrend` gives the current and next fiscal year EPS consensus, expected growth and the estimates 7, 30, 60 and 90 days ago, kept in `meta.json` `market.estimates` |

### Metrics and caveats

Each stock carries a `metrics` object that powers the catalog columns. Every metric of the registry that a stock has is a table column (none lives only in the screener), all visible by default: texts and flags (`altmanVariant`, `country`, `isAdr`, `isReit`, `isMine` as Yes or No) are text columns, `athDate` and the row dates `fundamentalsAsOf` (`FY As Of`) and `ttmAsOf` (`TTM As Of`) are date columns; the wide table scrolls inside its card. Percent values are plain numbers (`12.5` means 12.5%), ratios are plain numbers, money is USD

| Dividends | `dividendYield`, `dividendRate`, `dividendGrowth`, `dividendGrowth3y`, `dividendGrowth5y`, `dividendStreak`, `dividendCut10y`, `payoutRatio`, `fcfPayout` | Yield from the last 12 months of ex-dates over the last close; growth from calendar-year sums of split-adjusted dividends (last complete year, 1-year growth and 3 and 5-year CAGR); `scripts/metrics/dividends.ts`: `dividendStreak` = complete calendar years in a row with a higher dividend per share (`market.annualDps` keeps 30 complete calendar years plus the current one, so it reaches 29 at most), `dividendCut10y` = a drop of more than 10% in the last 10 complete years that the next year did not recover (a stopped dividend is a cut; a special dividend or a payment moved into the next year is not); payout and FCF payout are dividends paid over net income and over free cash flow of the latest fiscal year |

Inputs of the stage 5 stop factors and flags: `fcfNegativeYears` (fiscal years in a row with negative FCF), `cfoGrowth3y` (operating cash flow CAGR), `epsNiGrowthGap3y` (EPS CAGR - NI CAGR over 3 years, pp), `balanceYoyMaxChange` and `balanceYoyMaxLine` (the balance line, at least 10% of the assets, that changed the most year over year). | Growth | `revenueGrowth`, `epsGrowth`, `fcfGrowth`, `ebitdaGrowth`, `capexGrowth`, `assetsGrowth`, `debtGrowth` and the same with `3y` and `5y` | 1-year change relative to the absolute previous value; 3 and 5-year CAGR need two positive endpoints and consecutive fiscal years; EPS of a 10-K filer is the split-restated diluted EPS of the SEC layer (the same EPS `pegHist` reads), so a split between the endpoints does not read as a collapse |
| Margins | `netMargin`, `grossMargin`, `ebitdaMargin`, `fcfMargin`, and `...Delta`, `...Delta3y` | Latest fiscal year over revenue; deltas are percentage-point changes versus 1 and 3 fiscal years earlier |
| Performance | `perf1d`, `perf3d`, `perf1w`, `perfWtd`, `perf2w`, `perf20d`, `perf1m`, `perfMtd`, `perf40d`, `perf60d`, `perf1q`, `perfQtd`, `perf4m`, `perf6m`, `perf52w`, `perfYtd`, `perf1y`, `perf3y`, `perf5y`, `perf10y` | Cumulative price change from unadjusted closes up to `performanceAsOf`: `1d` to `60d`, `1w` (5 candles) and `2w` (10 candles) are rolling windows of N daily candles back from the last one; `1m` and `1q` take the candle closest to the same day 1 and 3 months earlier (a Saturday takes Friday, a Sunday Monday); `Wtd`, `Mtd`, `Qtd` and `Ytd` start at the last close before the current calendar week (Monday to Sunday), month, quarter (Q1 Jan-Mar, Q2 Apr-Jun, Q3 Jul-Sep, Q4 Oct-Dec) or year; `4m`, `6m`, `52w`, `1y` and longer go back to the last close on or before the same date that many weeks, months or years earlier (a month back from the 31st is the end of the shorter month) |
| Total return | `ytd`, `tr1y`, `tr3y`, `tr5y`, `tr10y`, `cagr3y`, `cagr5y`, `cagr10y`, `siAnn` | Cumulative and annualized return from dividend and split-adjusted closes; `siAnn` is since listing |
| Market | `price`, `week52High`, `week52Low`, `beta`, `sharesOutstanding` | Yahoo Finance; when the quoteSummary snapshot fails, `week52High` and `week52Low` are the highest and lowest close of the last 52 weeks of the chart |
| Price and swings | `pctFrom52wHigh`, `athPrice`, `athDate`, `athBasis`, `pctFromAth`, `volatility1y`, `sigmaFromMean60d`, `high60d`, `low60d` | Pure formulas in `scripts/metrics/swing.ts` over the full daily chart (the published history keeps daily rows for 31 days only), from closes adjusted for splits but not for dividends (Yahoo `close`, not `adjclose`): `pctFrom52wHigh` = (price / `week52High` - 1) x 100; `athPrice` is the highest close of the downloaded history with its `athDate`, `athBasis` names that history (`daily closes since <first date>`, the listing with the default `HISTORY_RANGE` of `max`), `pctFromAth` = (price / `athPrice` - 1) x 100; `volatility1y` = sample stdev of the last 252 daily log returns x sqrt(252) x 100 (needs 253 closes); `sigmaFromMean60d` = (price - mean of the last 60 closes) / their sample stdev; `high60d` and `low60d` are the highest and lowest of the last 60 closes. Too short a history or no spread gives null, never 0 |
| Foreign issuers | `isAdr`, `country`, `ttmIsAnnual` | `country` is the Yahoo `assetProfile.country`; `isAdr` is true for an issuer outside the US whose latest annual SEC report is a 20-F (no source flags an ADR as such, so New York registry shares such as ASML count too, 40-F filers do not); `ttmIsAnnual` is true when TTM is the last fiscal year because the issuer files no quarterly XBRL |
| Provenance | `returnsBasis`, `performanceAsOf` | Mandatory: how the returns were computed and the ISO date of the last price they are as of |

Caveats:

- Short horizons depend on the date of the last candle: a load during the trading session makes the last candle partial, and a stock whose `performanceAsOf` lags the others measures a different window, so rank them with Hide stale or a `Return As Of` filter
- Unavailable values stay empty and are never written as `0`; the one exception is a dividend yield of `0`, which honestly means no payment in the last 12 months
- Growth, margins and the balance ratios except `netDebtToEbitda` describe the latest fiscal year of the 10-K (`fundamentalsAsOf`); the SEC quarter metrics describe the trailing twelve months up to `ttmAsOf`; the row carries `fundamentalsBasis` (SEC, SEC with Yahoo gap fill, the 20-F or 40-F of a foreign issuer in us-gaap or ifrs-full with or without the gap fill, or Yahoo only)
- EBITDA is operating income plus depreciation and amortization (the combined D&A line, or depreciation plus intangible amortization when a filer tags them separately); it excludes non-operating income, so it can differ from Yahoo's EBITDA
- Data checks on every run (`fundamentals.checks` in `meta.json`, failures in the log and the step summary with a metric coverage table per sector): assets = liabilities + equity, gross profit = revenue - cost of revenue, four quarters = fiscal year (1% each, reported only); SEC TTM revenue more than 20% away from the Yahoo snapshot revenue is reported (revenue definitions differ, banks and insurers most of all); only a ratio above 5 or below 1/5 (`SCALE_ERROR_RATIO`, a unit or scale error such as ×1000) withholds that run's SEC values and keeps the published ones (marked `withheld` in the step summary)
- `index.json` and every `meta.json` carry `schemaVersion`; the feed only gains fields
- Banks, insurers and other issuers without an operating income, capital expenditures or a classified balance sheet have those metrics unavailable
- An annual-only TTM (`ttmIsAnnual`) whose fiscal year ended more than 12 months before the last close has no `pe`, `ps`, `pFcf`, `pb` and `evEbitda` (and nothing derived from that P/E, such as `forwardToTtmPe`)
- A debt-free issuer (no nonzero debt line tagged for 3 years) counts debt 0, so `evEbitda` and `netDebtToEbitda` (and a foreign issuer's `enterpriseValue`) have a value; a REIT without operating income still has no EBITDA. EPS of an issuer without a diluted EPS tag is net income over the SEC diluted shares of all classes (DCF on the ticker page), never over Yahoo's `sharesOutstanding`
- Foreign issuers with a fresh SEC layer (20-F, 40-F or a reporting currency other than USD) get `pe`, `ps`, `pFcf`, `pb`, `enterpriseValue` and `evEbitda` as market cap over the SEC amounts in USD (P/E = market cap / TTM net income), never price over EPS: EPS in a 20-F is per ordinary share, the price per ADR, and the ADR ratio is not in the SEC data. Growth and margins stay in the reporting currency so the exchange rate cannot bend a trend; `forwardPe`, `forwardPs`, `pePct5y` and `pFcfPct5y` stay empty for them. The run log and the step summary count the ADRs by source (`ADR fundamentals: N ADRs, SEC ifrs-full, SEC us-gaap, Yahoo fallback, none`). The scale check of an annual-only TTM allows 50% (Yahoo's revenue is a real TTM, growth alone moves them apart)
- ADRs and other issuers whose reporting currency differs from the price currency and that have no usable SEC layer keep growth and margins but lose every multiple that mixes the two currencies; their Fundamentals tab shows the annual statements in the reporting currency, named in the column headers (`Revenue (TWD)`) and in the subtitle, with no `$` on the amounts
- Share classes (GOOG and GOOGL, BRK-B) share one set of fundamentals and differ only in market data
- Price history is published as month-end rows with daily rows for the last 31 days; returns are computed from the full daily series before that sampling
- NYSE Arca has no stock rows in the SEC company table; Arca-listed stocks are not covered
- A stock whose Yahoo quote type is not `EQUITY` (ETFs, funds) is skipped; so is a security without a market cap (SPAC units and warrants, baby bonds, preferred shares) and a CIK that has no SEC company facts and no Yahoo statements (closed-end funds, trusts)
- SPAC shells and other new listings without a full fiscal year stay in the catalog with unavailable fundamentals; filter them by sector or industry
- The full feed holds about 6,500 stocks of the 7,157 SEC listings on Nasdaq, NYSE and Cboe (the rest are funds or have no market cap), about 80 KB each with the history pages (about 530 MB) plus a 25 MB `index.json` (3.8 KB per row, one company per line); it stays under the 1 GB GitHub Pages size limit. A local run of 250 stocks (`MAX_FETCHES=250`, `CONCURRENCY=24`) took 40 s, 6.25 stocks per second, so one 25-minute run reaches about 9,000 stocks and the first full fill takes one or two weekly runs (the GitHub runner can be slower; a run that hits the soft deadline saves the cursor and the next weekly run continues, the published index grows with every run). The update job of the workflow has a 45-minute timeout for the 25-minute updater plus the cache, build and the upload of the large artifact; since the feed is published as a Pages artifact and not committed, the Git history no longer grows with every run

### Update controls

Keep this table, `scripts/update-data.config.json`, `CONTROL_NAMES` and `--help` in sync

| Control | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --: | --- |
| `MAX_FETCHES` | `0` (all) | Batch size: with a positive value the updater stops after that many stocks and the next full-universe run continues after the cursor saved in `api/stocks/update-state.json` (restored with the feed, see [Publishing](#publishing)); empty or `0` is a full pass (a run that hits the 25-minute soft deadline also saves the cursor) |
| `REQUEST_SLEEP` | `1` | Minimum delay in seconds between request starts of one worker lane, including retries |
| `CONCURRENCY` | `24` | Number of parallel workers; each worker owns a request lane paced by `REQUEST_SLEEP`; 24 lanes refresh about 6 stocks per second (measured 6.25 locally), so the whole universe fits one or two 25-minute runs |
| `MAX_RETRIES` | `2` | Integer >= 1; retries after the initial request; only network errors and HTTP 403/408/425/429/5xx are retried with exponential backoff |
| `EXCHANGES` | `Nasdaq,NYSE,CBOE` | Listing exchanges to include, as named in the SEC company table |
| `TICKERS` | empty (all) | Space-, comma- or semicolon-separated ticker allowlist (`BRK.B` and `BRK-B` both work); blank means every stock listed on `EXCHANGES`; a run with `TICKERS` never reads or moves the rotation cursor |
| `MARKET_CAP` | `:` | Market cap range in USD (strict `min:max`); each bound may use `K`, `M`, `B` or `T`, for example `10B:` |
| `DIVIDEND_YIELD` | `:` | Dividend-yield percentage range |
| `HISTORY_RANGE` | `max` | Yahoo chart range for the price history (`max`, `10y`, `5y`, ...); a limited range is requested with explicit `period1` and `period2` |
| `HISTORY_PAGE_SIZE` | `1000` | Rows in each generated history JSON page |
| `SKIP_YAHOO` | `false` | Keep the published market data and history while refreshing SEC fundamentals |
| `SEC_UA` | `daggerok ETF feed daggerok@gmail.com` | SEC User-Agent override; SEC policy requires automated tools to declare a contact; the protected `SEC_UA` Actions variable wins when nonblank |
| `VERBOSE` | `false` | Print per-stock retry and fallback notices |
| `USE_SYSTEM_CA` | `auto` | TLS trust store: `auto` restarts the updater once with Bun's `--use-system-ca` when a request fails with an untrusted-certificate error; `true` always uses the system CA store; `false` never restarts. Not an individual workflow input: use `advanced`, the config file or the CLI environment |
| `PERFORMANCE_1D`, `_3D`, `_1W`, `_WTD`, `_2W`, `_20D`, `_1M`, `_MTD`, `_40D`, `_60D`, `_1Q`, `_QTD`, `_4M`, `_6M`, `_52W`, `_YTD`, `_1Y`, `_3Y`, `_5Y`, `_10Y` | `:` | Cumulative price change ranges in % (`min:max`), the windows of the `perf*` metrics; only `_YTD` to `_10Y` are individual workflow inputs (the workflow input limit), pass the short ones in `advanced`, for example `{"PERFORMANCE_20D": "10:"}` |
| `TOTAL_RETURN_YTD`, `_1Y`, `_3Y`, `_5Y`, `_10Y` | `:` | Cumulative total return ranges in % (`min:max`) |

`TICKERS` combines with the market cap, yield and return filters using AND logic; it does not override them. A stock without a value for an active range does not match it. Filtered or bounded runs (`TICKERS`, `MAX_FETCHES`, any range filter, `SKIP_YAHOO`) never shrink the feed: stocks that are not selected, are skipped by a filter or fail keep their published row and data files, and `api/stocks/index.json` always lists every stock that has a `companies/*/meta.json`. The one exception is a delisted stock: a run without `TICKERS` drops a row whose ticker is gone from the SEC exchange table and whose last price is older than 60 days (both conditions, so a transient gap in the table never drops a live stock) and prints it as `DELISTED`; its folder stays until `./scripts/update-data.ts --prune` removes it (run by hand about once a quarter). The default run covers the whole universe: every Nasdaq, NYSE and Cboe listing of the SEC table except preferred shares, warrants, rights and units (tickers ending in `-P*`, `-W*`, `-R*`, `-U*`) and except securities Yahoo reports without a market cap or as funds. Narrow it with `TICKERS` or a `MARKET_CAP` floor

### Examples

```bash
TICKERS="AAPL MSFT JPM XOM" ./scripts/update-data.ts
MAX_FETCHES=500 ./scripts/update-data.ts
MARKET_CAP="10B:" ./scripts/update-data.ts
DIVIDEND_YIELD="2:" TOTAL_RETURN_5Y="50:" ./scripts/update-data.ts
SKIP_YAHOO=true ./scripts/update-data.ts
./scripts/update-data.ts --signals   # the update, then api/stocks/signals.json against the index before the run
```

## TypeScript and verification

The browser app lives in `src/`: `index.html` (markup), `main.tsx` (TypeScript), `index.css` (Tailwind CSS v4 plus the app styles) and `favicon.ico`, built by Parcel into `dist` (`bun run build`, same setup as daggerok/csv and daggerok/options-desk) - no `tsconfig.json` needed. Bun runs the updater TypeScript out of the box

Verification before every publish:

```bash
bun install --frozen-lockfile
bun test
bun build --target=bun scripts/update-data.ts --outfile=/dev/null
git diff --check
```

`bun test` also covers the README controls table, the config file, `--help` and the workflow

### Screener core and user data

`src/core/` is the screener core, independent of the UI and free of DOM imports, so the browser app and Bun scripts share it; each module keeps its `*.test.ts` next to the code and `bun test` runs them too:

- `src/core/i18n/`: `t(key, params)` over the `en` and `ru` dictionaries, English fallback, the chosen language remembered in the browser
- `src/core/metrics/`: the metric registry, one entry for every key of `metrics` in this feed and in the 29 ETF brand feeds, with formula, unit, basis, source, direction and a name, short text and guide article in English and Russian; `bun src/core/metrics/check-feeds.ts api/stocks ../<Brand>/api` lists live feed keys without an entry
- `src/core/scoring/`: the stage 5 scoring over a screener universe: `config.json` (weights, archetype rules, statuses, stop factors, flags and Why notes in the preset format), its validation against the registry, the five scores, archetype, status, flags (`score.ts`) and the Why and flag texts in both languages (`why.ts`); `load-config.ts` reads the config in Bun for tests and the signals job
- `src/core/signals/`: the stage 8 signals: the previous and the new index scored and screened like the page (`signals.ts`: preset entries and exits, flags, status, archetype, score moves, quiet stocks, two-run exits, the restatement mark, 12-week retention), the thresholds (`config.ts`: score move 15, two runs, 84 days), the texts and the weekly issue body (`text.ts`) and the repo inputs of the updater (`load.ts`: base presets, `user-data/presets`, `user-data/peers.json`, the scoring config)
- `src/core/dcf/`: the DCF of plan stage 7: both flows, scenarios, margin of safety, the gap flag and the inputs read from `meta.json` (the tests repeat three hand calculations to the cent, one of them Alphabet)
- `src/core/research/`: TAM scenarios, the notes Markdown format with the nine questions, the promises tracker against the SEC quarters, and the repo and browser copies of these files
- `src/core/ticker/`: the statements with year-over-year changes, the long-run chart series and the peers table rows of the ticker page
- `src/core/similar/`: "Similar to X" (plan stage 6): the metric groups and starting values (`config.json`, schema and validator in `config.ts`, `load-config.ts` for the tests), percentiles in the sector or among a security type, the distance with pairwise exclusion of empty metrics, top hits with the main differences and the filter made from X (`similar.ts`)
- `src/core/screener/`: the engine (operators `>`, `>=`, `<`, `<=`, `=`, `range`, `in`; absolute, market, sector, industry, peers and history scopes with percentiles; missing as fail or skip; soft "N of M" mode; and, or, not expressions; funnel and intersections), the row adapter for stocks and ETFs, the base presets in `presets/*.json` with their `index.json`, own presets (canonical JSON, export file, import, share link, browser and repo copies in `own.ts`), peer groups (repo file plus browser overlay, export and import in `peers.ts`) and the reader of the ETF brand list (`etf-brands.ts`: parses the hub's `brands.json`, builds the feed URLs); `bun src/core/screener/bench.ts` measures it on a synthetic universe of 10,000 rows

`user-data/` holds the owner's public presets, peer groups, notes, TAM scenarios and promises, committed from browser exports (see `user-data/README.md`)
